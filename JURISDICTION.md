## THE CRITERION — one property: SUBJECT or APPARATUS

**An item is SEAT 3's when its outcome changes THE MEASURING APPARATUS — what counts as a
reading, what readings remain possible, or who is entitled to take one.**
**An item is SEAT 2's when it changes only THE SUBJECT — whether the system does correctly
what it already claims to do.**

### WHY THIS PROPERTY AND NOT ANOTHER
It is not invented; it is the soundness law restated as a routing rule. *A verifier that
can reconstruct the claim cannot verify it*, and *whoever is measured by a reading cannot
own it.* Seat 2 is measured by these readings. **Therefore Seat 2 may work on the subject
and must not own the apparatus.** Every candidate I tested that was NOT derived from
soundness — irreversibility, cost, blast radius, "is it risky" — failed, because each is a
MAGNITUDE rather than a PROPERTY, and magnitudes drift under pressure while properties do
not.

### THE TWO ANCHORS FALL OUT OF IT RATHER THAN BEING ADDED TO IT
  **"What counts as success"** is the apparatus's CALIBRATION. Seat 3's. ✓
  **"Anything that spends independence"** is the apparatus's VALIDITY — an import debited
  against a frame, a rotation, who occupies a seat. Seat 3's. ✓
Both are apparatus. The criterion generates the anchors instead of merely accommodating
them, which is the test I applied to it.

### THE SECONDARY DISTINCTION THE FIRST ONE NEEDS — REVERSIBILITY OF THE RECORD
Deleting CODE is reversible: git retains it, the apparatus is unchanged in any lasting way.
**Deleting or compacting RECORDS is not reversible, and it permanently narrows what any
future reading can say.** So:
  orphan deletion (code) -> SUBJECT -> Seat 2
  janitor compaction, frame retention, record truncation (records) -> APPARATUS -> Seat 3
This is why the janitor is Seat 3's even though it looks like housekeeping: folding
settlements to a tail would answer the performance question and **delete rung 3**.
